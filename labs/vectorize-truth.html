What this page is, and how to read each panel

The sibling page (golden corpus) compares the tracer to its own previous output. That catches drift, but it has no notion of correct — and its ±12% count bands actively forbid big improvements. This page scores against the authored SVG that produced the pixels: each case is rasterized, traced, and the recovered vectors are compared to the art itself. Every gate is an absolute distance from correct (0px boundary error, parsimony 1.0, every region recovered), so improvements move numbers down and nothing ever needs re-blessing. Nothing here reads or writes test/golden/trace-baseline.json.

Drag any box to pan, wheel to zoom; all boxes move together, so you can pin one detail across the truth, the trace and the error maps at once.

truth — the authored SVG. This is the answer sheet, not another guess.
raster input — the SVG rasterized at the chosen size; the only thing the tracer sees. Changing the raster size and watching the numbers stay flat is the scale-stability check.
boundary overlay — authored boundary in green, traced in magenta. Where the tracer is right they overprint; where it is wrong you see one colour alone.
miss heat — the authored boundary, coloured by how far the nearest traced boundary is. Hot = the tracer missed that arc.
invented heat — the traced boundary, coloured by distance to the nearest authored boundary. Hot = the tracer invented an edge the art does not have.
dropped regions — red marks a flat region the composited art contains and the trace does not. This is the failure raster fidelity is blind to: merging a small low-contrast region into its neighbour barely moves ΔE or SSIM while destroying the topology.
Rasterizer note. This page rasterizes the SVG with the browser canvas; the Node runner (src/devtest/groundTruthRun.ts) uses resvg. Anti-aliasing differs slightly between them, so numbers here can differ from the CLI in the last decimal. The corpus, the trace options, the gates and the scoring functions are the same modules in both (truthCorpus.ts, geomScore.ts).
